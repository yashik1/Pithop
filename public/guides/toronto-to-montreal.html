Toronto to Montreal: make the drive part of the trip

The quickest route is usually the 401, but a good stop can turn a long stretch of highway into a day with a waterfront walk, a historic site or a scenic detour. Pick one or two stops that match your schedule instead of trying to see everything.

Stops to consider along the way

These are planning ideas, not a promise that every place is directly beside your route. Use the map links to check the location and let your route planner calculate the real detour from your chosen road.

Waterfront · short break

1. Kingston waterfront

Kingston makes a useful leg-stretching break around the middle of the drive. Walk by the harbour, grab food downtown, or turn the stop into a longer visit if you want to explore the historic city. It is a practical choice when your group needs a proper break rather than another highway service area.

Scenic drive · flexible time

2. Thousand Islands Parkway and Gananoque area

East of Kingston, the Thousand Islands region is a strong choice if scenery is the point of the trip. The parkway offers a more leisurely alternative through part of the region; Gananoque is a convenient base for a walk or a seasonal boat excursion. Check seasonal schedules before building a boat tour into a tight itinerary.

History · longer visit

3. Upper Canada Village

Near Morrisburg, this living-history site can be a memorable stop for travellers who prefer interpreters, heritage buildings and a planned visit over a quick photo break. It is not an on-and-off-the-highway stop: check the seasonal calendar, admission details and the extra time required before committing.

Small-town break

4. Brockville or a St. Lawrence riverside walk

If you want a simpler pause, Brockville offers a downtown and waterfront break without turning the day into a full attraction visit. Keep this flexible: choose a café, a walk or a meal based on how the drive is going.

How to choose the right stops

Only have 30–60 minutes?

Prioritize a waterfront walk, coffee or viewpoint close to your route. Avoid attractions with timed entry or long walking loops.

Have half a day?

Choose one anchor visit—such as a heritage site or a boat excursion—and keep the rest of the plan flexible.

Travelling with kids?

Alternate one planned attraction with a low-pressure outdoor break. A shorter, reliable stop often beats squeezing in a second ticketed venue.

Before you go: travel time, construction, seasonal opening dates and weather change. Confirm admission and hours directly with each attraction, then recheck the route on departure day.
